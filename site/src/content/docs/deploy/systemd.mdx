On a Linux machine without containers, systemd runs services: it starts them at boot, restarts them when they fail, and keeps their output in the journal. The service here is [the one for containers](/deploy/docker/), built with `cargo build --release`: it stops cleanly on the SIGTERM systemd sends.

The files are in the repository's `examples/deploy/systemd/`.

## The unit

<Example file="examples/deploy/systemd/shop.service" />

- **Log lines** go to the journal. Its output isn't a terminal, so they're JSON, ready for a shipper that reads the journal. For lines that read well in `journalctl -u shop`, set `OXTEL_LOG_FORMAT=text`; the journal adds its own timestamp beside oxtel's.
- **Stopping.** systemd sends SIGTERM and waits `TimeoutStopSec` before killing the service. The service stops at once, after sending what's pending, which waits up to 5 seconds when the receiver doesn't answer, so keep the timeout above that.
- **A user of its own.** `DynamicUser` runs the service as a user made for it, and the `Protect` settings keep it from writing anywhere. oxtel needs no files of its own.

## Its settings

Settings that differ from machine to machine, such as where telemetry goes and the key it needs, go in the environment file the unit names. Only root reads it, so a key in it stays private:

<Example file="examples/deploy/systemd/telemetry.env" />

The file's endpoint expects a Collector on the same machine, which is a common setup: one Collector per machine, sending on to your [backends](/backends/), and holding their keys. Point it at a backend directly the same way.

## How it's checked

On every change, CI installs the service on its Linux runner with these files, after `systemd-analyze verify` checks the unit, and runs a Collector on `localhost:4318`. It starts the service, sends a request, and stops it with `systemctl stop`. The service must stop cleanly within seconds, its last line in the journal must say it stopped, and the request's span must reach the Collector. `check.sh`, in `examples/deploy/`, has the checks.
